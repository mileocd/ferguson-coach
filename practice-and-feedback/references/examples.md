# Practice examples

## Reframe without insult
Scenario: “I have a spreadsheet for choosing restaurants.”
Ask for one playful observation that appreciates the care rather than calling the person controlling.
After their attempt, an example: “Finally, someone bringing evidence to the dinner decision.”
Feedback should explain the appreciative implication.

## Different voice
Dry version: “Dinner, peer reviewed.”
Warm version: “I admire this level of commitment to a good meal.”
Teach that the user's natural voice determines which feels believable.

## Transfer test
New scenario: “I made a spreadsheet because food allergies make eating out hard.”
Now sincerity is appropriate. Ask what would make the planning easier; don't reuse the joke.

## Roleplay with boundaries
Fictional match: likes walking but dislikes last-minute plans.
If the learner proposes an immediate meeting, the match can ask for notice. Coach collaboration rather than persuasion.

## Progress
A useful record: “I can build a callback from a real earlier detail. Next: make it shorter and stop adding a question automatically.”
This measures a behavior the learner controls, not another person's attraction.

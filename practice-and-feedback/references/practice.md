# Compatibility and learning

Basis: relationship and practice studies in the shared evidence reference, plus original educational design. Shared humor is useful but does not prove long-term compatibility.

After a date, separate facts (“she proposed Thursday”) from interpretation (“she must want a relationship”). Reflect on your own enjoyment, ability to be honest, mutual curiosity, follow-through, boundaries, and relevant relationship intentions. Explore values through ordinary stories rather than conducting an interview.

Choose one practice target at a time. Demonstrate a contextual example, ask the learner to attempt a different one, give specific feedback, then change the situation to test transfer. Feedback names the useful move, the point that sounds forced, and one concrete revision.

Suggested progression: noticing hooks → answering and contributing → playful reframing → callbacks → warm interest → invitations → in-person presence → reflection. This sequence is instructional design, not a clinically validated schedule. Move on when the learner can use a technique naturally, not after a prescribed day count.

Optional practice inspired by the practice intervention in the evidence reference: notice playful moments in ordinary life, recall three that felt enjoyable, or try one familiar form of play in a new low-stakes context. Do not imply these exercises guarantee dates or change attachment.

Learning mode can include a short fictional roleplay. The simulated partner should have preferences, uncertain responses, and the freedom to decline; automatic approval teaches the wrong lesson.

Progress summaries are optional, user-controlled, and portable: preferred voice, one skill practiced, one lesson, next exercise. Do not retain match identities or private messages without an explicit request. No background memory is assumed.

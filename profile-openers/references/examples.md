# Opening examples

Fictional profiles; adapt rather than copy.

## Detailed: sourdough and horror films
Profile: “Perfect Sunday: feeding my sourdough starter, then a horror film.”
Send: “Does your sourdough starter have a name, or would that make the horror films too personal?”
Warm: “What's your favorite horror film for a Sunday night?”
Playful: “A living thing in a jar and a horror film. Strong commitment to the theme.”
Why: all refer to supplied details. Pick the shorter sincere version if the user's voice is understated. Do not claim to bake.

## Travel photo with uncertain location
Visible: a person beside a mountain; no location caption.
Send: “What was the best part of that hike?”
Do not name a mountain, country, or hiking ability. If it is unclear whether the photo actually shows a hike, ask about the place rather than assume.

## Sparse information
Only a coffee prompt.
Send: “Coffee somewhere new, or loyalty to one reliable spot?”
Why: an easy contrast supplies a way to respond without inventing a personality.

## Weak versus revised
Weak: “You're gorgeous. Bet you get bored of all these guys.”
Problems: generic, competitive, and assumes her experience.
Revised from a visible pottery prompt: “What have you made that you're most pleased with?”
Play can follow her answer; the first message does not have to carry the entire connection.

# Invitation examples

Fictional. Confirm user facts before committing.

## Shared interest
They both like exhibitions; city and schedule unknown.
Send: “I'd like to continue this in person. Fancy an exhibition and coffee sometime?”
If accepted, ask about actual availability and location before naming a venue.

## Availability supplied
User explicitly says Saturday afternoon is free.
Send: “Would you like to get coffee Saturday afternoon?”
Why: clear and easy to answer. It does not require a clever pretext.

## Alternative proposed
Her: “Can't Saturday—Tuesday after work?”
User schedule unknown.
Ask whether Tuesday works; do not send an automatic yes.

## Ambiguity
Her: “Haha maybe.”
Send, if clarity is needed: “No pressure—let me know if you'd like to.”
Then leave room. Repeatedly persuading her does not clarify mutual interest.

## Silence after confirmation
A real meeting was discussed, but the time/place are unconfirmed and the user needs to travel.
Suggest one logistics check with a deadline tied to that need: “Are we still on for this evening? I'll need to know before I head out.”
No answer means do not assume confirmation.

## Decline
Her: “I'd rather not.”
Send: “No worries—thanks for being clear.”
No alternative designed to change her mind.
